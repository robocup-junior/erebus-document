Your robot has a battery, and the game ends when it runs out. On this page, you learn how fast it drains, how your program can read it, and how to replace it.

## How the battery works

- The battery starts at 100% and **drains with the speed of the wheels**: the faster they turn, the faster it drains. With the default settings, a stopped robot uses no battery.
- In **swamps**, it drains faster.
- To replace it, the robot must **stay still on a contact tile for 5 seconds**. The battery is then at 100% again. Moving, or leaving the tile, starts the 5 seconds again.
- If the battery reaches 0%, the game ends with `Battery depleted`. An empty battery cannot be replaced.

The control window shows the battery level above the timers. Below 20%, it shows "Low battery":

![The score, the battery level and the timers in the control window, with a low battery](./battery-low.png)

The rules do not say how fast the battery drains: teams are expected to find it out by testing. With the default settings of Erebus v27, two wheels at full speed (6.28 rad/s) drain about 1.25% per second, so a robot that never stops would run out in less than a minute and a half, much less than the 8 minutes of a game. Organizers can change these settings, so measure them in your program rather than relying on these numbers.

## Replacing the battery

Contact tiles are cyan, so the [colour sensor](/docs/programming/sensors/colour-sensor/) sees them: red is close to 0, green and blue are high.

## Planning
- Know where the contact tiles are: remember them while you explore, as you do for the map.
- Before the battery gets low, head for the nearest contact tile you know. Leave a margin, since the way back can take longer than you expect.
- Avoid swamps when you can, or cross them quickly.
- Stopping costs game time, but no battery with the default settings, so the 1 second stop to report a wall token is free for the battery.
